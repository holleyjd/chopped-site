In a Chopped (or Guillotine) league there are no matchups. Every week the lowest-scoring team is eliminated, its players go to waivers, and everyone else moves on. The last team standing wins.

That single rule changes almost every decision you make. In a head-to-head league you need to beat one random opponent. In a Chopped league you need to beat **one team: the worst one**. You are not trying to finish first in a given week. You are trying to not finish last.

## The cut line is lower than it feels

With 18 teams in Week 1, the score that gets chopped is usually far below the league median. You can have a bad week, finish 14th, and be completely safe. New Chopped players tend to manage as if every week is a must-win, chasing ceiling plays they don't need.

Look at your league's actual cut lines. The [League Tracker](/tools/league-tracker) shows the lowest surviving score each week and how far above it each team finished. If you cleared the cut line by 25 points last week, a small downgrade at one position is not what eliminates you. A zero at one position is.

## Floor beats ceiling, until it doesn't

The goal each week is to minimize your chance of finishing last. That leads to a simple rule of thumb:

- **If you project comfortably above the cut line, reduce variance.** Start the steady 12-point receiver over the boom-or-bust deep threat. Avoid players with injury questions in late games. Your enemy is the disaster week, not the average one.
- **If you project at or near the bottom, increase variance.** A safe lineup that projects 10th-lowest out of 10 remaining teams loses most of the time. Your only path is a high-ceiling lineup that might jump two or three teams.

Most managers spend most weeks in the first bucket. That's why "boring" lineups win Chopped leagues more often than they win head-to-head leagues.

## Zeros are the real killer

The fastest way to get chopped is not a bad game. It's a player who doesn't play: a late scratch, a bye you forgot, a quarterback benched at halftime. A single zero can cost you 15 to 25 points against a cut line that's often decided by fewer than 5.

Practical habits that prevent zeros:

- **Check inactives before every kickoff window,** not only Sunday morning. Thursday and Monday players get forgotten.
- **Avoid late-game players with questionable tags** unless you have a same-position backup in an even later game.
- **Plan bye weeks two to three weeks out.** Waivers in Chopped leagues are expensive, so finding a bye fill-in the week of is the costliest way to do it.
- **Roster a backup at your thinnest position** rather than a third high-upside stash. Depth has more value here than in any other format.

## The league gets stronger every week

Each chopped roster dumps its players back into the pool. The best surviving teams grab the best of those players, so the remaining rosters improve over time while the field shrinks. A score that was safe in Week 3 is often the score that gets you chopped in Week 12.

Two things follow from that:

1. **Your roster has to improve too, not just hold.** Treat each week's waiver run as a chance to upgrade a starter, not just patch a hole.
2. **Don't anchor on early-season cut lines.** Track the trend. If the lowest surviving score has climbed from 85 to 110 over six weeks, plan for 115 next week, not 85.

## The middle of the season is where leagues are decided

The early weeks reward avoiding mistakes. The late weeks are a contest between a handful of strong rosters. The middle third is where the big decisions happen: the strongest teams get chopped through bad luck, their stars hit waivers, and whoever has the FAAB and the roster flexibility to absorb those players becomes the favorite.

That's why budget management is the other half of Chopped strategy. We cover it in [FAAB in Chopped leagues: when to go big and when to hold](/strategy/faab-aggressive-vs-safe).

## Quick checklist

- Know last week's cut line and your margin above it.
- Projected well above the line: lower variance. Near the line: raise it.
- Eliminate zeros before chasing points.
- Plan byes two to three weeks ahead.
- Expect the cut line to rise all season.
