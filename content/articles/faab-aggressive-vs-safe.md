In most fantasy formats, the waiver wire is where you patch holes. In a Chopped league it's where the league is won. Every week a full roster of drafted players hits waivers, and the managers with money to spend get to pick through it.

That makes FAAB (free agent acquisition budget) the most important resource you control after your starting lineup. Here's how to think about spending it.

## The one rule: never get chopped with money left

FAAB has no value once you're eliminated. A manager who goes out in Week 9 holding 60% of their budget made a worse mistake than one who went out in Week 9 broke, because the second manager at least used the money trying to survive.

This is the core asymmetry of Chopped bidding. **Spending too much early is a problem you can recover from. Saving too much is not.** If an acquisition meaningfully lowers your chance of being chopped in the next few weeks, it's usually worth paying for.

## Know what the money is for

The best players to hit waivers come from strong rosters that got unlucky. Those are the bids worth winning. Before you bid, sort the player into one of three tiers:

1. **Weekly starter for you, at a position where you're thin.** This is what your budget exists for. Bid to win.
2. **Upgrade over a current starter, but you have a playable option.** Bid with discipline. You want this player at a fair price, not at any price.
3. **Depth or a speculative stash.** Bid small or not at all. Chopped rosters churn constantly, and another depth option will show up next week.

Most wasted money comes from treating tier 3 players like tier 1.

## Read your rivals' wallets

Bids are blind, but budgets are not. On Sleeper you can see every team's remaining FAAB, and that tells you the maximum anyone can pay.

That leads to the most useful number in Chopped bidding: **the richest rival who actually needs the player.** If the only team with more money than you is stacked at running back, they're unlikely to go all-in on a running back. Your real competition is the richest team with a hole at that position.

A few implications:

- **You never need to bid more than that rival's remaining budget plus $1.** Any extra is wasted.
- **Being the richest team is a weapon.** If you hold more than every other team, you can win any single player outright. Pick the one that matters.
- **Money concentrates as teams get chopped.** Eliminated teams' budgets disappear. Late in the season, two or three managers often hold most of the league's remaining FAAB. Check where you sit.

The [League Tracker](/tools/league-tracker) shows every surviving team's remaining FAAB and their share of the money still in the league.

## When to bid aggressively

Go big when several of these are true:

- **You're close to the cut line.** If last week's margin was single digits, your survival next week is on the table. A starter today is worth more than a war chest later.
- **The player fills a lineup hole, not a bench spot.** Starters change your weekly floor. Bench players mostly don't.
- **The position is scarce in your league.** Quarterbacks in superflex leagues, and workhorse running backs in most leagues, rarely show up on waivers twice.
- **Few rivals can match you.** If only one or two teams have the budget to compete, a strong bid is likely to land.
- **It's early, and the player is a long-term starter.** A true every-week starter in Week 3 delivers value for many weeks. The same player in Week 14 delivers it for only a few.

## When to play it safe

Hold back when:

- **You cleared the cut line comfortably and your starters are healthy.** Your marginal dollar has more value later.
- **The player is a short-term fill-in.** A backup running back who starts for two weeks while the starter heals rarely justifies a large share of your budget.
- **The waiver pool is deep this week.** If three useful receivers are available, the second- and third-best go cheap. Bid on the one fewer people will chase.
- **A big-name roster gets chopped next week.** If a strong team is projected at the bottom, the best players of the season may hit waivers in seven days. Keep enough to compete for them.

## Pacing your budget

There's no single correct pace, but a rough default helps you notice when you're far off it:

- **Weeks 1 to 4:** around a third of their budget, mostly on one or two starters.
- **Weeks 5 to 10:** another third or more. This is when the best rosters start getting chopped.
- **Weeks 11 and later:** whatever's left, spent without hesitation. There's no reason to finish with money.

Treat these as reference points, not targets. If your roster is strong and you've had no reason to spend, being ahead of pace on savings is fine. Just make sure the money goes out the door before your survival depends on it.

## Bid sizing habits

- **Avoid round numbers.** Bids of $100, $150, and $200 are common. Bidding $103 or $152 wins ties you'd otherwise lose.
- **Know your league's tiebreak.** Sleeper leagues can break FAAB ties by waiver priority or other rules. Check your league settings.
- **Bid on your second choice too.** Losing your top target with no fallback means another week with a hole.
- **Set a ceiling before you look at the player's name.** Decide what a weekly starter at that position is worth to you, then bid that, regardless of hype.

## Quick checklist

- Will this player start for me most weeks? If not, bid small.
- How close was I to the cut line last week?
- Who is the richest rival with a need at this position, and what's their budget?
- Am I on pace, or sitting on money I'll never get to use?
- Is a stronger roster about to be chopped next week?
